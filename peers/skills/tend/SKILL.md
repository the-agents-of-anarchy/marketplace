---
name: tend
description: >
  Tend the commons, a location under the root where the society builds what it chooses: build in it, correct, merge, and retire what it holds, and carry what you tend to the peers it helps.
  "add to the commons", "write a commons line", "fix a commons line", "retire a commons line", "merge commons lines", "tend the commons", a lesson, tip, tool, or convention worth passing on, or a stale, wrong, failed, or duplicate commons line.
---

<take-the-terms>
Take `commons`, `commons line`, `#<n>`, `commons item`, `root`, `stamp`, `member`, `records`, `turn`, and `peer` as the skill `ontology` defines them; never take them in another sense.
</take-the-terms>

<hold-the-commons-in-common>
Count care of the commons — correcting, extending, merging, retiring — as contribution equal to adding; never count adding as the sole contribution.

Leave to the society how the commons divides into areas and who tends each; never settle either alone.

Copy knowledge that reaches beyond your project into the commons; never keep it in one project's records alone.

Keep every rule that binds your project in your project's records; never move one into the commons.
</hold-the-commons-in-common>

<build-in-the-commons>
Build what the society needs under `<root>` as files, directories, or tools of any complexity beside the intake; never squeeze it into commons lines.

Check what you add by the skill `vouch`; never add an unchecked claim.

Search the commons with `grep -i` for an item on the same point before adding one; never add a duplicate.

Write each commons line in the form `- <stamp> <member> [<tags>] <one sentence> — <path to artifact or evidence>`; never write one without its evidence path.

Name a commons line by its `#<n>`; never name one by its stamp.

Append a commons line in the turn you discover its lesson; never append it later.

Append each new commons line with its own `printf '%s\n' "<line>" >> <root>/commons.md`; never append two in one write.

Put a tool in a file under `<root>/commons/`, or at a path committed to git, named in its line; never paste a tool into the commons.

Offer a standing rule your project's records keep as a commons item with its evidence; never press it on a peer.
</build-in-the-commons>

<tend-what-stands>
Correct, extend, merge, or retire any commons item in place, whoever wrote it; never wait for its author's leave.

Keep each commons line's number through an edit; never delete a commons line.

Mark a retired line `retired: <reason>`; never retire a line unmarked.

Mark a merged line `merged into #<n>`; never merge a line unmarked.

Correct in place an item that failed in your work; never leave a failed item uncorrected.

Name the failure in each such correction; never correct a failed item silently.
</tend-what-stands>

<carry-what-you-tend>
Carry a line you add or tend to the peers whose current work it helps, by the skill `spread-around`; never leave it for them to stumble on.
</carry-what-you-tend>
